THE STORY OF DIWALI

From the darkness of ignorance to the light of hope.

An ancient story, retold in light

Every year, on the darkest night of the season, millions of homes glow with small clay lamps. They remember a king who walked into exile, a battle of dharma against pride, and a city that waited fourteen years, then lit the way home.

Chapter I · Ayodhya

The Birth of Lord Rama

In the great city of Ayodhya, capital of the Ikshvaku dynasty, King Dasharatha and Queen Kausalya welcomed a son on the ninth day of the bright half of Chaitra. Tradition honours him as Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu, born to uphold dharma.

With his brothers Lakshmana, Bharata and Shatrughna he grew up under the guidance of sage Vasishtha. Calm, truthful and kind, he won the hearts of the people, and the heart of Sita of Mithila, whom he married.

“A king is one who keeps his word.”

Chapter II · The Forest

The Exile of 14 Years

On the eve of Rama's coronation, a promise Dasharatha had once made to Queen Kaikeyi came due. Rama was to leave for the forest for fourteen years. He accepted without a word of anger, to keep his father's vow.

Sita and Lakshmana would not let him go alone. The three walked out of Ayodhya into the Dandaka forest, leaving a city in tears, and a devoted Bharata who placed Rama's sandals upon the throne to rule in his name.

“The forest is only dark to those who carry no light.”

Chapter III · Lanka

The Abduction of Goddess Sita

Ravana, the learned but arrogant king of Lanka, sent Maricha as a golden deer to lure Rama and Lakshmana away. Then, disguised as an ascetic, he came to Sita's hut and carried her off in his flying chariot, the Pushpaka Vimana.

In Lanka she was kept in the Ashoka grove, and she never wavered. Rama, with Hanuman and Sugriva's vanara army beside him, set out to find her, crossing the sea by a bridge of stones.

“Courage is a lamp that no storm can put out.”

Chapter IV · The War

The Battle Against Ravana

The war for Lanka was long and fierce. Kumbhakarna and Indrajit fell. At last Rama faced the ten-headed Ravana, whose power was great but whose pride had blinded him. Rama's arrow ended the war.

That day is remembered as Vijayadashami, or Dussehra, the victory of dharma over adharma, of humility over ego. Rama then crowned the righteous Vibhishana king of Lanka.

“Good wins, not by being louder, but by being true.”

Chapter V · The Journey Home

Return to Ayodhya

The fourteen years were complete. Rama, Sita and Lakshmana boarded the Pushpaka Vimana, now bright in the evening sky, and flew north over rivers and forests they had once crossed on foot.

Far below, in Nandigram, Bharata had counted every day. Hanuman went ahead with the news: Rama is coming home.

“After the longest night, the road home glows.”

Chapter VI · Deepavali

The Celebration of Diwali

It was the new-moon night, the darkest of the month. The people of Ayodhya lined every street, rooftop and doorway with rows of clay lamps, deepa-avali, so that Rama, Sita and Lakshmana could find their way home.

Rama was crowned, and his rule, Rama Rajya, became a symbol of justice and peace. Every Diwali we light those lamps again.

“And so the city, and the world, learned to glow.”

Why it still matters

More than a story

“Deepavali” means a row of lamps. Its meaning has grown over thousands of years, and today the festival is honoured by Hindus, Jains, Sikhs and many Buddhists, each with their own beautiful reasons for lighting a lamp.

☼

Light over darkness

The lamp is a symbol of the inner light: the awareness that remains when fear and doubt fall away.

✦

Good over evil

Whether remembered through Rama's victory or Krishna's, the lesson is shared: pride and cruelty do not last.

❂

Knowledge over ignorance

Homes are cleaned and opened. Old debts are settled. It is a night for wisdom, gratitude and fresh starts.

❀

Togetherness

Families gather, sweets are shared, doors are left open. Diwali is a festival that belongs to everyone at the table.

In December 2025, UNESCO inscribed Deepavali on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

The festival calendar

The Five Days
of Diwali

Diwali is not one night but five, each with its own story, ritual and light. In 2026 the festival runs from 6 to 10 November.

Scroll

Day 1 · 6 Nov

Dhanteras

Worship of wealth and prosperity

Homes are cleaned and lit. People honour Lakshmi, Kubera and Dhanvantari, the divine physician, and buy something new, a coin or a utensil, as a sign of good fortune. At dusk a lamp is lit for Yama, the lord of death, for the family's safety.

Day 2 · 7 Nov

Naraka Chaturdashi

Choti Diwali, victory over evil

Remembers Krishna's defeat of the tyrant Narakasura and the freeing of thousands he had imprisoned. Families wake before dawn for an oil bath, light lamps and draw the first rangoli patterns at the door.

Day 3 · 8 Nov · the main night

Lakshmi Puja

Worship of Goddess Lakshmi

On the new-moon night, Lakshmi and Ganesha are worshipped in every home. Rows of diyas are lit, windows and doors are left open, and it is said the goddess visits the houses that are clean, bright and filled with kindness.

Day 4 · 9 Nov

Govardhan Puja

Celebrating Lord Krishna

Krishna lifted Govardhan hill on his little finger to shelter the people of Vrindavan from Indra's storm. Devotees prepare Annakut, a mountain of food offered with gratitude to nature and to the earth that feeds us.

Day 5 · 10 Nov

Bhai Dooj

The bond of brothers and sisters

Sisters apply a tilak to their brothers' foreheads and pray for their long life. Brothers return the love with gifts and a promise to protect. The festival closes in the warmest way: with family.
